Before the move I pulled up a map to see how far I was really going, and noticed something I'd never bothered to check. Palma sits at 39.57° north. Laurel, Maryland, sits at 39.10°. If you walked due west from the cathedral in Palma and somehow kept your feet dry, you'd pass about thirty miles north of where I'm unpacking now.[^1]

Same latitude means the same sun. The longest day of the year is the same length in both places, within a few minutes. Winter afternoons go dark at the same point in the season. On paper, nothing about the light should feel different.

It does, though. Mallorca's light comes off limestone and the Mediterranean, which is clear and salty and has almost no tide.[^2] Maryland's comes through humidity and trees, off a bay that's brackish, brown-green, and moves twice a day.

> [!quote]
> “Same angle of sun, completely different material for it to land on.”

## Six hours

The bigger change is the clock. Palma runs six hours ahead of the East Coast, and for years my working day was split around that gap: mornings for Spain, afternoons and evenings for clients in New York.[^3] Now the gap runs the other way. The people I call before breakfast are the ones I used to call after dinner.

I spent years in the Navy standing watches, where the day is cut into four-hour pieces and nobody much cares whether it's light outside. It made me bad at noticing seasons. Moving along a line of latitude, instead of across it, turns out to be a good way to start noticing again.

So: same sun, different ocean, six hours. I'll write more as the boxes get emptied.

[^1]: A degree of latitude is roughly 69 miles, so half a degree is a long afternoon's drive.

[^2]: A few inches, most days. The Chesapeake moves about a foot.

[^3]: Except for a week or two each spring and fall, when Europe and the US change their clocks on different dates and the gap shrinks to five hours.
